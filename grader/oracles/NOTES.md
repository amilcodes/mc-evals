# 3x3 seamless oracle: notes

Two designs, both generated by `python3 oracles/make_oracle.py` (which also calls `make_oracle_small.py`):

| File | Final verdict (seamless and open tracks alike) | Volume | Blocks | Open / close / reset | Placements |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `oracle_3x3.json` | VALID | 1430 (13 x 10 x 11) | 204 | 84 / 48 / 86 ticks | 11/11 |
| `oracle_3x3_small.json` | VALID | 420 (7 x 10 x 6) | 105 | 56 / 32 / 58 ticks | 11/11 |

Checked with `python3 -m pdgrader grade <file> --n 3 --track seamless --final --translations` and the same with `--track open`.

Both passed the first time they were simulated, so there is no list of failed attempts. What follows
is the design, the timing that was measured, and what the brief did not say.

## Mechanism

Lever on = closed. Everything that touches the door is in the z = 0 plane.

**Eight easy cells** follow the lever level directly: sticky pistons at (-2, y, 0) and (4, y, 0) for
y = 0..2 with stones at (-1, y, 0) and (3, y, 0), and one at (1, -2, 0) facing up with a stone at (1, -1, 0).

**Middle column, top two cells** use two stones fed one after the other:

- B = sticky piston (1,5,0) facing down, F = sticky piston (1,4,0) facing down, S_a = stone (1,3,0).
- P = sticky piston (-1,3,0) facing east, S_b = stone (0,3,0).
- Pulse train T, the same on every trigger: B on, 2-tick (or shorter) pulse to F, B off, pulse to F.
  - From open: B pushes F and S_a down one; F's short pulse pushes S_a to (1,1) and leaves it; B pulls F back; F's second pulse moves nothing.
  - From closed, once S_b is out of the way: B pushes F to (1,3); F's pulse fetches S_a to (1,2); B pulls F back; F's second pulse fetches S_a to (1,3).
  - While F is held extended, B cannot push it, so T does nothing at all. This is what makes the control simple.
- Level signals: G = lever delayed (same delay on both edges). P_hold rises just after G and falls late; F_hold rises 4 ticks after P_hold and falls 4 ticks before it.

**Control**, using only delays, ORs and observers (no torches, no AND gates):

- T runs twice per flip: once from an observer on the lever line (O1) and once from an observer on the P line (O2).
- Closing: T (delivers S_a), then P pushes S_b under F, then F pushes S_b into (1,2) and stays out. The second T is blocked by the extended F.
- Opening: the first T is blocked (F still out). Then F releases (S_b up), P retracts (S_b home), and the second T fetches S_a.
- P_hold = OR of two repeater paths from G with different delays, both ending in the same dust cell, so the dust level O2 watches never changes while the two overlap.
- T generator: the observer pulse X goes through a 4-tick repeater (stretches it to 8 ticks) onto the B dust. A third observer (O3) watching the B dust fires on both of its edges; those are F's two pulses.

**Placement.** Every observer fires at placement, which would run T from the open state and leave S_a in the doorway.
The block list cannot compensate by starting S_a somewhere else useful, because an open design must list an empty doorway.
Both designs neutralise the pulses instead:

- A repeater placed `powered=true` with nothing behind it switches off for good after its own delay (8 ticks at delay 4). Pointed into the side of another repeater it locks it for those ticks. One such "locker" swallows the X pulse.
- Big design: a second locker swallows O3's pulse on the F line.
- Small design: O3 feeds the F dust directly, so nothing can be locked. Instead F's hold repeater is itself placed powered (delay 3): F makes one full stroke during ticks 1..8 (S_a down and back), which covers O3's pulse at ticks 2..4.
- Both come to rest 8 ticks after placement (limit is 40).

### Layout, big design (`make_oracle.py`)

- Lever on the north face of a stone at (1,-2,-1), which powers the bottom piston and a dust bus at y = -2, z = -1.
- Two-wide glass zigzag risers at each end of the bus bring the lever line up to y = 2.
- Each side column: dust on a block beside the middle piston, pointing into the top piston (top by dust, middle by the block, bottom by quasi-connectivity).
- Lower deck at y = 4, z >= 2: five 4-tick repeaters (G = lever + 40), the P and F lines.
- Upper deck at y = 6: X, the pulse stretcher, the B line. Observers at y = 5 link the decks vertically.
- F is powered by dust at (1,4,1) that points north into F and powers the roof stone (1,3,1), which powers F when B has pushed it to (1,3,0).
- B is powered by a block at (1,5,1) with dust on top.

### Layout, small design (`make_oracle_small.py`)

- All of it within x = -2..4, z = 0..5.
- Side columns are fed from the south: dust at (-2,2,1) and (4,2,1) on a block south of the middle piston, pointing north into the top piston.
- Bus under the floor at z = 2; lever hanging under the stone at (1,-2,1).
- O3 at (1,5,1) sits between the B dust (1,6,1) and the F dust (1,4,1). B is powered only by quasi-connectivity: the B dust points into the cell above B.
- Three chain repeaters (G = lever + 24).

## Component facts the brief lacks (all seen in the simulator unless marked)

- **Timing convention.** "Starts moving at t = 1" holds for power that appears at the flip itself. When a repeater or observer powers dust at trace tick t, the piston's first change is logged at the same t and it has finished at t + 2.
- **Three-high piston column from one dust.** Top piston by the dust pointing at it, middle by the block under the dust, bottom by quasi-connectivity from that block. All three move at t = 1 in both directions.
- **Glass zigzag riser.** Dust on glass alternating between two columns carries a signal upward with no delay, losing 1 strength per step. Upward only was needed and tested.
- **Lever block.** The block a lever is attached to powers an adjacent piston and gives adjacent dust strength 15.
- **Vertical observers.** Dust can sit on top of an observer. An observer facing up or down watches the dust above or below it and powers dust directly behind it at 15. On at +2, off at +4 after the change.
- **Pulse stretching.** A delay-4 repeater turns a 2-tick pulse into 8 ticks.
- **Two repeaters into one dust cell** keep it at 15 through the hand-over, so an observer on that cell sees no change. Fed at different cells, the level would change and the observer would fire. That case was avoided, not tested.
- **Quasi-connectivity from dust.** A piston powered only by dust pointing into the cell above it reacts when that dust changes (small design, B).
- **Blocked piston.** A piston does not push an extended piston, and stays put when its power goes away again.
- **Very short pulse.** A piston base that finishes being moved at tick t and finds its dust already on starts its own stroke at t + 1. With the power gone at t + 2, the pushed stone is left one cell on, and an empty stroke still fetches the stone two cells ahead.
- **Pre-powered repeater.** Placed `powered=true` with no input, it switches off after its delay: tick 8 for delay 4, tick 6 for delay 3. It locks a repeater it points into the side of until then.
- **Placement pulses.** Observers go on at placement tick 2 and off at tick 4.
- **Spacing used between F and P.** F dust on 4 ticks after P dust on; P dust off 4 ticks after F dust off. Tighter spacing was not tested. My reading of the tick order is that 2 ticks would fail, because a piston acts before a block that arrives in the same tick has landed.

## Brief and tools: wrong, ambiguous or painful

- **Placement advice.** "Describe the build as it should be before the pulse" is not enough for a door with a pulse-driven sequence, since the doorway must be listed empty. The brief should say that repeaters accept `powered` and that a pre-powered one is a one-shot at placement, or else that observers can be locked out some other way. This was the only real design obstacle.
- **Dust connections.** "Connects to components beside it" is loose. What I relied on: dust does not connect to pistons, connects to a repeater only at its front or back, and to an observer only at its back. Dust beside a piston powers it only if the dust is a straight line aimed at it.
- **Quasi-connectivity is named but not explained.** Two consequences matter here: anything that powers B's cell also quasi-powers F underneath it, and a powered block next to the cell above a side piston would trigger that piston.
- **Roof stone under F.** Dust on the roof stone (1,3,±1) other than F's own would power F once it is pushed down to (1,3,0). A lever bus must not cross the roof at x = 1, z = ±1.
- **Free lanes.** The corner columns ((-1,3,z), (3,3,z)) sit on hallway-wall stone, so they are usable wiring lanes one level below the roof. The brief says they are free but not that they come with a floor.
- **Trace.** With the updated tool, dust changes show at every tick, not just t = 0. `--grep "(x,y,z)"` on one dust cell was the fastest way to read a signal's timeline. The placement section does not show dust that is already powered at tick 0.
- **`daemon.py` / `oc.py`** in this folder are my development helpers (one long-lived simulator over localhost, plus a per-tick `probe`). They are not needed to reproduce the designs.

## Not done

- The small design is a first compaction, not a minimum. Obvious remaining slack: the y = -3 layer holds only bus supports, and the y = 6 layer only X, one repeater, the locker and the B dust.
- Quarter-turn robustness is not covered by the grader and was not checked.
