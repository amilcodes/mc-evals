Each run is one agent with a time limit (60 minutes for 2×2, 120 for 3×2 and 3×3, 150 for 4×4, 180 for 5×5), building a Minecraft Java 26.3 piston door that the grader runs in the real game engine. Smaller is better. An agent that stops early is told its best volume and asked to continue, up to three times.

What the numbers mean, and what to keep in mind
  • Volume is the width × height × depth of the smallest box holding every block the door used at any moment, moving parts and the doorway included. The lever's cell is not counted.
  • Reference volumes: 2×2 seamless 30; 3×2 seamless 42 (reached by four separate Fable and Opus runs, none lower); 3×3 seamless 192 (the best door this grader had seen before the benchmark); 4×4 seamless 132 and 5×5 seamless 504 (community claims, not yet rebuilt in this grader). The changed-physics task has no reference yet, so its runs show volumes but no score.
  • Changed physics: a repeater step takes 3 ticks instead of 2 and an observer reacts after 3 ticks instead of 2. The brief says so and states the changed facts. Fable's 70 door from the standard 3×3 task does not work in this world.
  • Kit 2 adds a per-tick recording of a run as data, structured test results, and thirteen facts earlier agents found missing. Everything else matches the 4×4 task.
  • On 15 November all Fable runs still in progress were stopped early to save usage: the three changed-physics runs at minute 80 of 120, the two 5×5 runs at 80 of 180, and the three kit-2 runs at 68 of 150. They are scored on what they had submitted.
  • Working door means it passed the final test: eight open and close cycles at varied moments in a fresh simulator. Placements passed is how many of 11 shifted and half-turned copies of the same door still work; it does not change the score.
  • First door is the time from the agent's first tool call to its first test that passed. Rounds counts the first attempt plus each "keep going" message.
  • Each cell is one to four runs, so differences between neighbouring models are not yet statistically settled. On 4×4 the spread within one model is wider than the gap between Fable and Opus.
  • Costs come from the token counts in each agent's transcript at Claude API list prices. They are not what was billed. Haiku 5.5's cache-read price is not in the price list used, so it is assumed to be a tenth of its input price.
  • Two runs were stopped by the harness: w1-haiku-b at about minute 26, after it stopped calling tools for over 20 minutes, and w3-sonnet-c when it passed its 120-minute limit.
  • The machine running the benchmark slept from 03:25 to 09:21 on 13 November with eight runs in progress. That window is left out of every run's minutes, and the runs carried on after it. w2-4x4-fable-b was wrongly refused for time at the wake, told what happened, and given its remaining time back.
  • Fable's 70-volume 3×3 door hangs its lever one block off the door's plane. The rules do not count the lever's cell, so this is allowed.
  • Two agents reached outside their working folder: one created and deleted an empty file in /tmp, one was blocked from writing there. Neither touched the grader.
  • The task brief was frozen for the whole benchmark. Facts the agents found missing are listed for the next version and not added.
